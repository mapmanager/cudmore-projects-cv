
## Independent-review prompt

Act as the director of an academic imaging core or a senior faculty member in a biology department. You have received an unsolicited cover letter and CV from someone asking whether your institution could benefit from additional scientific software expertise. No position has been advertised.

Evaluate the attached cover letter and CV using only information stated in those two documents.

Important constraints:

- Do not use previous conversations, stored information, personal knowledge about the applicant, or facts inferred from outside sources.
- Do not search the web.
- Do not assume that an omitted qualification or accomplishment exists.
- Distinguish problems in the documents from practical constraints the applicant cannot resolve in a cover letter, such as whether your institution currently has funding.
- Evaluate the documents from the recipient’s perspective, not the applicant’s.
- Do not rewrite the documents unless explicitly asked. Recommend focused changes instead.

Critique the following dimensions:

1. Immediate clarity: After a brief initial reading, who is this person, what do they offer, and what are they asking the institution to consider?
2. Audience fit: Are the CV and letter appropriate for an academic imaging core and experimental biology researchers?
3. Value proposition: Is the proposed relationship between an imaging core and an analysis-core capability clear and compelling?
4. Role clarity: Is it clear whether the applicant wants to join an existing organization in a staff role or expects the institution to create a separate core?
5. Service orientation: Does the applicant appear genuinely interested in researcher support, troubleshooting, documentation, and training, as well as software creation?
6. Cross-domain applicability: Does the application explain how experience in neuroscience, vascular biology, and cardiac physiology could transfer to other biological research areas without making unsupported claims?
7. Scientific and technical credibility: Are important claims supported by concrete experience, software projects, documentation, or working applications?
8. Accessibility: Is the software terminology understandable to biologists and imaging-core personnel who may not be software engineers?
9. Focus and flow: Identify repetition, unnecessary detail, abrupt transitions, mixed messages, or material better placed in the CV than the cover letter.
10. Tone: Does the application sound collaborative, realistic, service-oriented, and confident without sounding promotional or presumptuous?
11. Practical response: What questions or concerns would you have before arranging a conversation?
12. Overall effectiveness: Would you respond to this outreach? Explain why or why not.

Use this response structure:

- Initial 30-second impression
- Strongest aspects
- Top concerns, ranked by importance
- Evidence from the documents supporting each concern
- Questions you would ask the applicant
- Focused recommended changes
- Material that should remain unchanged
- Final decision: likely to respond, uncertain, or unlikely to respond

Evaluate the cover letter and CV both separately and as a combined application packet.
