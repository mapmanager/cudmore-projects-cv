I am writing to introduce myself and ask whether my combination of experimental science, quantitative analysis, and research software engineering could contribute to the work of your laboratory, department, or research community. I have designed experiments, built custom acquisition systems, developed real-time visualization and analysis software, interpreted results, and published the findings. I work closely with experimental scientists to connect research questions with computational methods. An immediate need within one project—such as loading a proprietary data format, organizing an imaging series, or developing a quantitative measurement—often reveals a problem shared across laboratories. I build reusable computational backends, graphical applications, and scripting interfaces so that these solutions can support current experiments while providing a foundation for future projects.

This approach can contribute at several levels. Within a laboratory, I can collaborate on experimental design, acquisition, analysis, interpretation, and publication. Across a department or center, I can help identify shared analytical needs, develop sustainable software, train researchers, and provide continuity as projects and personnel change. I enjoy the practical work that makes these efforts successful, including troubleshooting datasets and pipelines, improving documentation, mentoring scientists and engineers, and turning recurring support problems into reusable improvements.

I also design publication and data sharing into the analysis workflow. My goal is for the data, metadata, completed analyses, and software used during research to remain connected through publication and reuse. This makes analyses easier to inspect and reproduce while reducing the need to reconstruct workflows at the end of a project.
